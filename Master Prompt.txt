Yes. Your problem statement is now specific enough to turn into a **complete implementation-grade master prompt** for an AI coding/engineering agent.

The key architectural decision should remain: **this must not be built as a generic pregnancy chatbot**. The intended system is a personalized pipeline of **user profile → pregnancy context → knowledge retrieval → personalization → safety validation → AI response → evidence/sources**. 

The system also needs separate knowledge domains—modern medical, Ayurvedic, food/regional, and lifestyle—and the source/evidence metadata must remain attached to the knowledge.  

Below is the **master prompt I would give to a senior AI software-engineering agent**.

## How I would actually build this

The master prompt above is intentionally comprehensive, but **do not ask an AI coding agent to generate all of it at once**. That is one of the easiest ways to get a beautiful-looking but broken project.

Use this implementation sequence:

### Phase 1 — Foundation

```text
Next.js
     ↓
FastAPI
     ↓
PostgreSQL
     ↓
pgvector
     ↓
Docker
```

First prove:

```text
Frontend → Backend → Database
```

works.

### Phase 2 — Knowledge engineering

This is arguably the most important part of your project.

Your source architecture should look like:

```text
                KNOWLEDGE
                    │
       ┌────────────┼────────────┐
       ↓            ↓            ↓
   Medical       Ayurveda     Nutrition
       │            │            │
       └────────────┼────────────┘
                    ↓
              Lifestyle
                    │
                    ↓
             Regional/Cultural
                    │
                    ↓
             Source Registry
                    │
                    ↓
               Chunking
                    │
                    ↓
               Embeddings
                    │
                    ↓
                pgvector
```

Your original technical specification correctly emphasizes that the knowledge base should be **curated rather than random internet information**. 

And for Ayurveda, don't just store a paragraph called `"ayurveda.txt"`. Preserve provenance such as source, book, chapter, verse/page, original text, translation, and interpretation where available.
